The Roslindale Bus Lane — The 4pm Problem

August 2026 · Boston Transit

Here is an analysis of the Washington street corridor bus lane system using the MBTA historical bus dataset. All analysis is AI assisted but Luke Winslow finalized.

Takeaways

  • 4pm is the worst hour on the corridor, regardless of direction.
  • Two thirds of that 4pm penalty is the school year, not the commute. Strip out summer and the median drops from 7.2 minutes to 5.5. Strip the weekday out entirely (Saturday) and it's 4.6.
  • Where a lane already exists, the PM outbound lane back to Roslindale Square, 2pm to 7pm, it still isn't decoupling buses from traffic. 88% of trips run more than two minutes over free flow.
  • I can't cleanly prove the lane's own impact from the data, and I wish I could. The switch on/off test finds nothing at the lane boundaries, most likely because the lane hours are wide enough that congestion has already arrived or already left by the time the boundary hits. That's a real limitation, not a dodge.
  • I suspect much of the slowdown is an enforcement problem. There is a clear experiment here: Enforce the lane that already exists in the PM, measure the same segment before and after. Let's find out how many person-years of time can be saved through a handful of tickets and some towing around 2PM?

What's actually slow

The morning inbound lane is the best performing window on the corridor, but it's lost a quarter of its speed since 2022, and only a fifth of that is explained by ridership or dwell changes. The afternoon is worse across the board, and the worst single hour, 4pm inbound, has no protection at all. Two thirds of that 4pm penalty is term time, school dismissal traffic and the kids riding these buses, not the 5pm commute peak, which barely moves.

The lane that exists doesn't decouple buses from traffic

That's the part that should worry anyone who wants transit to work. The PM outbound lane already has legal force from 2PM to 7pm, and buses in it are still stuck. 88% of trips run over free flow. The median trip takes more than twice free flow; one in ten takes three times. A lane that fails nine trips in ten isn't being kept clear.

I can't cleanly prove the lane's impact from the data, and I wish I could

The cleanest test, does travel time step when the lane switches on or off, finds nothing at either boundary. That's not proof the lane does nothing. It's more likely that lane hours are set wide enough that congestion has already arrived, or already left, by the time the boundary hits, so there's nothing left for the test to see. The before/after comparison from when the outbound lane was painted in 2021 is a cleaner signal, a real 1 to 2 minute improvement, but it's tangled up with pandemic era traffic recovery, so I'd call it suggestive, not conclusive.

This is an experiment, not a guess

We don't need another study to know whether enforcement works here. We can just find out. Via whatever means necessary, turn up enforcement pressure in the PM period (Camera, focused staffing). Increased enforcement, all else equal, and measure the same segment before and after. That's the whole design. If it works, we'll have a real number for what enforcement buys, not a projection. This is what effective government looks like, try the cheap, reversible thing first, measure it honestly, and only then argue about what to build next.

What I'd actually do

  1. EXPERIMENT: Enforce the PM outbound lane first. Forest Hills to Roslindale Square, 2pm to 7pm. It already exists, it already has legal force, and it's still failing 88% of trips. No new paint, no parking removal, no hearings, just enforcement of a lane that's already there, and a before/after measurement of what enforcement bought. This is the highest rider hour opportunity on the corridor.
  2. FUTURE: Extend the inbound lane into the afternoon, during the school year. 4pm inbound is the worst hour on the corridor and has no lane at all. Traffic on Washington isn't just unidirectional commuting, the school year effect proves that, so a term time only afternoon inbound lane targets the actual worst hour without picking a summer fight nobody needs.

The morning lane is real but marginal by comparison. Lower baseline traffic means less to recover even with perfect enforcement. Start where the rider hours are, and start with something we can actually measure.
